## Before and after contact
Altitude decreases during descent as the aircraft transitions to landing configuration. In this example airspeed approaches 145 kt. After touchdown speed decreases, braking appears and the spoilers deploy. Compare those changes with the contact event on the shared timeline.
Reaching an expected IAS alone does not establish a stabilized approach. Inspect vertical path, configuration, thrust and recorded valid LOC/GS deviations as well. The FD switch state is not a substitute for guidance commands.

## Limitations and follow-up
The demonstration recording uses a coarse **20 s** interval. The −120 ft/min and 1.18 g values describe a prepared sample, not peak touchdown loads. They cannot reliably identify flare timing, maximum acceleration or an exact touchdown point.
- Zoom IAS/V/S/pitch charts around contact.
- Check source and validity of AP/A/T, LOC and GS states.
- Compare the contact position on the map with plan and actual track.
- Use denser recording and before/after context for a real assessment.
**Demonstration conclusion:** the recording illustrates event order and configuration, but does not support a complete landing-quality assessment.
